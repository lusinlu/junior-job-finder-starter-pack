# 🚀 Kickstart Your ML/Engineering Job Search

This guide is for aspiring ML engineers, software developers, and technical professionals looking to land their first (or next) role in tech. Whether you're a fresh graduate or switching fields, this resource walks you through practical, high-impact steps you *must* take to stand out — starting with your CV.

---

## 📄 Cruft Your Resume

Before anything else, you need a clean, ATS-friendly CV. We've added a LaTeX-based template (used by thousands, including myself) that you can edit directly on Overleaf.

### ✅ How to Use the CV Template
1. **Create an Overleaf Account**: [Sign up here](https://www.overleaf.com)
2. **Open the Template**: Use the provided `.tex` file (in this repo) and upload it to Overleaf.
3. **Fill in Your Real Data**: Replace the placeholders with actual, *concise* and *relevant* experience.
4. **Use a PDF Export**: Once done, download your resume as a PDF to apply online.

Clean formatting, clear structure, and no fluff. Keep it simple and sharp.

---

## 🤖 Automatic Resume Scoring Systems

Most resumes don’t even reach a human. They're first filtered by an AI system — called an Applicant Tracking System (ATS). These systems check for:

- **Keywords** from the job description
- Relevant **experience**
- **Education** & **certifications**
- Tools, languages, and platforms used

### 💡 Pro Tips
- Use **exact phrasing** from job descriptions. If a job says *"TensorFlow"*, don’t just write *"deep learning tools"*. Spell it out.
- Tailor your CV to **each job**. Yes, it's a bit of work. But it *will* increase your chances of passing the first round.

## ✅ Check Your Resume

Use these tools to test your resume against real job descriptions. They give you a match score and offer optimization tips.

- [Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co) – Free limited scans
- [Resumeworded](https://resumeworded.com) – AI scoring + LinkedIn feedback
- [SkillSyncer](https://www.skillsyncer.com) – Free with registration

Run your CV through at least one of these **before** submitting applications.

## 🧑‍💻 Open Source Contributions

Let’s be honest: if you're passionate about ML or software development, you should already be building things.

- Contribute to open-source projects
- Solve real problems with code
- Push your projects to a public GitHub

Start small — fix a typo in a README, submit a bug fix, or clone and build your own versions of cool ideas.

Your GitHub is your **technical portfolio**. Own it.

---

## 🌐 Build Your Network

You don’t need to go viral, but having a professional online presence helps.

- **Create a LinkedIn profile** with a clean headshot, title (e.g. *"Machine Learning Engineer | Python | Computer Vision"*)
- Connect with **HRs, engineers, and alumni** from companies you’re targeting
- Engage with content from your field

You’ll be surprised how far a few thoughtful DMs and posts can go.

---

## 📚 Publications (Bonus for AI/ML Roles)

If you're serious about ML/AI, a **peer-reviewed publication** (conference or journal) can significantly boost your profile. It shows:

- Deep technical knowledge
- Research ability
- Writing and analytical skills

It’s not required — but if you’ve done academic work, *highlight it* in your resume and LinkedIn.

## 🎯 Final Thoughts

Getting your first ML/dev job is a process. But if you:

- Craft a clean resume
- Pass ATS filters
- Show real work on GitHub
- Build a solid network
- And optionally publish or contribute...

You’re already ahead of 90% of applicants.
